Selling

Selling your home in North San Diego County

What a sale actually nets, what pricing turns on here, and which pre-sale work earns its cost back — before you commit to anything.

What will I actually net selling my home in San Diego County?

A San Diego County seller's net is the sale price minus the mortgage payoff, commissions, county and city transfer tax, escrow and title fees, any Mello-Roos or HOA owed through close, and whatever the buyer negotiates after inspection. The gap between the headline price and the wire that lands is routinely six figures, and it is knowable in advance.

City transfer tax is worth flagging early because it is not uniform across the county, and inspection credits are the line most sellers forget to plan for. A net sheet built before listing — not after an offer — is what turns those from surprises into decisions.

How should I price my home in North San Diego County?

Pricing a North San Diego County home starts with the specifics an automated estimate cannot see: which Mello-Roos district the parcel sits in, which school boundary the street falls on, whether a view is protected by topography or by nothing, and what the Coastal Zone permits on that lot.

Those four factors move price more than square footage does in the communities we work, and they are why two apparently identical homes trade at different numbers. See the Mello-Roos lookup for the tax half of it.

Should I renovate before selling in San Diego County?

Some San Diego County pre-sale work returns more than it costs and some does not, and the split is local rather than general. Compass Concierge exists to fund the work that does — the brokerage fronts the cost and is repaid at closing, so a seller is not choosing between their savings and their sale price.

The judgement is which work qualifies. Paint, flooring, landscaping and staging usually earn their cost back; structural and mechanical work usually does not, and is better disclosed and priced in. Terms change, so ask for the current ones. More on Concierge.

When is the best time to sell in San Diego County?

San Diego County has a genuine spring selling season, but the more useful answer for any individual seller is that competing inventory in their own price band matters more than the month. Six similar homes listed on the same street is a harder market than February ever was.

Current active and pending counts for a specific neighborhood and price band come from the MLS on request. No figure is published here because it would be stale within weeks, and a stale number is worse than none.
