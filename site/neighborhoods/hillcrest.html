What follows is the part of a Hillcrest search that is hard to look up: which tax districts apply, which district assigns the schools, and what we have actually done here. Every figure names its source.

Does Hillcrest have Mello-Roos, and how much?

No community facilities district in the County Auditor's active FY 2025-26 list is named for Hillcrest. That is the documented basis for saying Hillcrest homes generally do not carry Mello-Roos — though it describes district names, not a guarantee for any particular parcel.

No community facilities district in the active list is named for Hillcrest. Maintenance assessment districts — a different, smaller instrument — do operate in San Diego's urban neighborhoods, and the tax bill itemizes any that apply.

A Mello-Roos amount is specific to the parcel, not the neighborhood: it varies by district, improvement area and the phase a home was built in. The authoritative figure is the line item on the property tax bill, which names the district and gives a contact number.

Source: County of San Diego Auditor & Controller, Active Mello-Roos Districts (CFD) for FY 2025-2026, retrieved 2026-07-25.

What school district serves Hillcrest?

Hillcrest is served by San Diego Unified School District. Attendance is assigned by address rather than by ZIP code, so two homes a few streets apart can feed different schools — confirm the specific address with the district before relying on it.

What did the 2024 Hillcrest plan amendment change?

San Diego’s City Council adopted the Hillcrest Focused Plan Amendment on July 30, 2024, creating capacity for roughly 17,000 additional homes with densities that allow high-rise construction — a generational rezoning of the neighborhood’s core.

A plan sets capacity; construction follows financing and takes years — so the amendment’s near-term effect is on land value and development interest, not on next year’s skyline. For owners the practical questions are parcel-specific: what density now applies to this lot, and what is proposed nearby. The city’s plan page and development tracker answer both.

What is happening to the UCSD hospital campus in Hillcrest?

UC San Diego is redeveloping its Hillcrest medical campus under a long-range development plan — a multi-year rebuild that keeps the institution in the neighborhood while replacing most of the aging site — and construction phasing there is a years-long fact of life for the surrounding blocks.

Institutional campuses run on published plans: the LRDP and its environmental documents state what gets built, roughly when, and how traffic and staging are handled. A buyer near the campus reads those documents rather than guessing — the difference between a construction season and a construction decade is in them.

What kind of housing stock does Hillcrest actually have?

Hillcrest layers a century of stock — early 1900s streetcar-suburb houses, mid-century apartment courts, 1970s–80s condo conversions, and newer mixed-use buildings — and each era carries its own inspection and ownership questions.

Conversion-era condos deserve particular attention to HOA reserves and building systems — a 1970s building’s pipes do not care about its renovated kitchens. On the older houses, the usual pre-war items — foundations, laterals, original wiring — apply as they do across the urban core.
