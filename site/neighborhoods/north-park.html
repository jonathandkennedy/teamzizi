North Park Real Estate Guide

What follows is the part of a North Park search that is hard to look up: which tax districts apply, which district assigns the schools, and what we have actually done here. Every figure names its source.

Does North Park have Mello-Roos, and how much?

No community facilities district in the County Auditor's active FY 2025-26 list is named for North Park. That is the documented basis for saying North Park homes generally do not carry Mello-Roos — though it describes district names, not a guarantee for any particular parcel.

No community facilities district in the active list is named for North Park. Where a home is a designated historic resource, the relevant tax program runs the other way — a Mills Act contract can reduce property taxes in exchange for preservation obligations.

A Mello-Roos amount is specific to the parcel, not the neighborhood: it varies by district, improvement area and the phase a home was built in. The authoritative figure is the line item on the property tax bill, which names the district and gives a contact number.

Source: County of San Diego Auditor & Controller, Active Mello-Roos Districts (CFD) for FY 2025-2026, retrieved 2026-07-25.

What school district serves North Park?

North Park is served by San Diego Unified School District. Attendance is assigned by address rather than by ZIP code, so two homes a few streets apart can feed different schools — confirm the specific address with the district before relying on it.

Do North Park homes fall under historic-district rules?

Parts of North Park sit inside designated historic districts — the Dryden District among them — where City of San Diego historical-resource rules attach to contributing homes: exterior changes route through historic review, and Mills Act contracts can reduce property taxes in exchange for preservation obligations.

Historic status is parcel-specific and checkable before an offer: the city’s historical resources records state whether a home is designated or contributing, and escrow will surface an existing Mills Act contract — an obligation and a tax benefit that transfer with the house. Neither is a surprise anyone needs to have.

Why do North Park lots carry so many ADUs?

North Park’s transit-adjacent zoning made it one of the City of San Diego’s most active areas for accessory dwelling units under the city’s bonus rules — and those rules have been revised more than once, so what a lot could build last year, this year and next are three different questions.

The current municipal code, not the neighbors’ lot, is the authority on what an ADU plan can be — and for a buyer, existing unpermitted units are the thing to smoke out in inspection and permit history. The ADU rules explainer covers the state layer and how to check a specific parcel.

What does buying a North Park Craftsman actually involve?

North Park’s signature stock is pre-war — Craftsman and California bungalows from the 1910s and 1920s — which makes foundations, original electrical, sewer laterals and a century of renovation-over-renovation the substance of an inspection there.

Permit history is the shortcut: a bungalow with documented system renewals is a different risk from an undocumented flip, at the same list price. And where a home might qualify as a historic resource, that status changes the renovation path — check it before planning, not after.

Check the record: official North Park sources

Every figure above names its source. These are the official pages where those records actually live — verified August 2026, and worth more than any summary of them, ours included.

  • North Park Community Plan — the land-use plan for Greater North Park, historic resources included.
  • San Diego Unified — School Finder — the district's own address lookup for attendance boundaries — the authority on which school an address feeds.
  • City Treasurer — short-term rental (STRO) program — license tiers, current availability counts and the rules that govern any rental under one month.
